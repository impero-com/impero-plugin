# Risks and controls

For the risks in a risk directory, the controls that mitigate them, and what happened to them.

- **What Impero holds.** A risk directory, such as a register of financial reporting risks, holds processes and the risks under them. Its risk entities, such as a country or a business unit, are its own, apart from the entities controls are assigned to, even with the same name. A risk is shared by the directory's entities or local to one, and is rated, responded to, linked to controls and assessed at each entity on its own: `impero_list_risks` gives one row per risk per risk entity.
- **Ratings and response.** The inherent impact and likelihood rate a risk before anything is done about it. The response is one of Impero's five, in the directory's own words: Reduce, Accept, Transfer, Avoid or Not applicable. Under Reduce, the residual impact and likelihood are rated on their own; with Accept or no response, the residual is the inherent; with Transfer, Avoid or Not applicable, there is none. Impero applies this rule, and the rows give its result. A formula column, such as a risk level, is worked out from the ratings, as Impero's page works it out.
- **Mitigating controls and coverage.** Controls are linked to a risk at an entity under Reduce. Each link can have a coverage: a whole-number percentage a person typed, their estimate of how much of the risk that control mitigates. Impero doesn't measure it, add the figures up or check them against 100. Its own figure is the average over the controls that have one. Impero's page shows a control with no figure as 0%, so a 0% there often means nobody has entered one.
- **Assessed:** someone marked the risk assessed at that entity, and the row says who and when. An assessed risk is locked until it's unassessed, so `Not assessed` with who and when it was last assessed usually means it's being changed.
- **"Do my controls cover my risks?"** Find the directory and the entity with `impero_list_risk_directories`, then list the risks there with `impero_list_risks`. Name the risks under Reduce with their controls, each control's coverage as typed and Impero's average, and those with no control. Impero doesn't rate whether a risk is mitigated enough: that's the customer's criteria, or the person's, so give the figures as Impero records them.
- **From a control:** `impero_search` for its id, then `impero_list_risks` with `control_id`, which reads every risk directory the person can see.
- **One risk's story:** `impero_list_risks` for its ids, then `impero_get_risk_history`.
- **A risk's file:** `impero_list_risks` with `detail: full` for its `file_id`, then `impero_get_risk_file`. Read it as *Files* says.
- **Between two dates:** `impero_compare_risks`, for a whole directory, as Impero's Insights tab compares it.
